About

Stop with the slop. Get to the point.

LLMs work in two directions. Summarizing is lossy compression: a long input becomes a short one, and detail is lost. Expanding a short prompt into a long output inflates it with invented detail.

Slop Free is an experiment to find a prompt that minimizes loss in compression and reduces inflated detail. What is left is text that gets to the point.

  1. 1

    Evaluate

    Each week a new candidate prompt is tested against an accuracy and efficiency standard.

  2. 2

    Test

    The candidate is then tested against the golden prompt in the quiz. Humans test the readability of the output by answering 5 short quiz questions.

  3. 3

    Rank

    If the candidate prompt outperforms the golden prompt in readability, it replaces the old prompt.

  4. 4

    Repeat

    Indefinitely.

How to use it

Paste it or save it. Doesn't matter. Just use it.

Include the golden prompt in your agent and witness the magic. Less filler and more directness. Fewer walls of AI text.

For one message

Write your request, leave a blank line, paste the prompt.

Explain how a heat pump keeps a house warm at -20°C.

Give the reader the essentials in as few words as possible.
- Open with what the subject is and why it matters. ...

For every chat

Save it as a personal preference, a custom instruction in your agent, or embed it in your system prompt.

The end of your system prompt. Always fetch the latest: api.slopfree.ca/v1/golden (JSON) or slopfree.ca/golden.txt (plain text)

Tips

  • Keep it last. Put task-specific instructions before it, so the golden prompt is the final thing the model reads.
  • Re-paste in long chats. Models drift back to old habits as a conversation grows. Pasting it again resets them.
  • Edit freely. Mix it, mash it. Do whatever you want. Please contribute any prompt that you think works better.

    Human First Design

    Crafting for humans, with AI

    1. 1

      Design for the human first.

      Most end users are people, and forcing them to sort through a wall of AI output is a bad experience.

    2. 2

      Protect the negative space.

      Leave things out so the real point stands out. Human attention is small, and AI output is big. Edit. Edit. Edit.

    3. 3

      The burden is on the designer.

      You, as the designer or writer, hold the context. It’s your responsibility to make the output understandable. Do not push the responsibility onto the reader to decode a confusing design or sift through a wall of text to find the point.

    4. 4

      Make it obvious.

      Each paragraph should make its point obvious, as should each next step in an application. A user or reader shouldn’t have to guess what’s going on, and it should be easy to tell when something is wrong. Errors should give enough information to correct the problem, or at minimum enough to support troubleshooting.

    5. 5

      Peel the onion.

      Show the essence first, then give the user a path to dig deeper. In writing, lead with the most important points and follow with the necessary details.

    6. 6

      Respect their time.

      Just because we can create at light speed doesn’t mean anyone has to consume it. It’s still the user’s or reader’s choice. You have to earn the right for someone to spend time reading your doc or using your system.

    7. 7

      Never accept the first output.

      Treat the first output, whether text or an application, as a draft. Edit it heavily using the principles above. Remember: put the human first.
